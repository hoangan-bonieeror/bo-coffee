> [!NOTE]
> **In short:** Arabica is more aromatic and gently acidic; Robusta is bolder and more bitter, with more caffeine, and easier to grow. Both can be delicious when grown, processed and roasted with care.

## 1. Quick comparison

| | Arabica (*Coffea arabica*) | Robusta (*Coffea canephora*) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caffeine in the bean | About 1.2–1.5% | About 2.2–2.7% |
| Typical taste | Bright acidity, floral, fruity, sweet | Bold, bitter, dark chocolate, nutty, heavy body |
| Natural sweetness | More sugars | Fewer sugars |
| Growing | High, cool altitudes; more disease-prone | Low, hot regions; hardier and more pest-resistant |
| Share of world production | Most of the rest | About 35–40% |

Caffeine and production figures from [GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/robusta-coffee) and [Coffee Chronicler](https://coffeechronicler.com/robusta-vs-arabica/); sources give slightly different ranges.

## 2. Caffeine isn't flavor

Robusta has nearly twice the caffeine, and caffeine is bitter — but most of the bitterness you taste comes from roasting. A very dark Arabica can taste more bitter than a medium-roast Robusta.

## 3. Vietnam and Robusta

Vietnam is the world's second-largest coffee producer after Brazil, and nearly all of its output is Robusta ([GCR Magazine](https://www.gcrmag.com/the-rise-of-robusta-coffee)). The Central Highlands — Dak Lak, Gia Lai, Lam Dong — are the heart of it. Vietnamese Arabica covers far less land, mostly in highland areas such as Cau Dat (Lam Dong) and Son La.

High caffeine and a heavy body make Robusta a natural fit for the traditional way of drinking: a strong phin brew with condensed milk and ice.

## 4. Fine Robusta

For a long time Robusta was seen as cheap filler for instant coffee and blends. Lately, more farms pick only ripe cherries and process them as carefully as specialty Arabica. The result: sweet, clean Robustas with chocolate, roasted-nut and dried-fruit notes — well worth trying if you only know Robusta from café coffee.

## 5. Which to choose

- **With milk, strong, in a phin:** Robusta or a blend with Robusta.
- **Black, pour-over, bright and aromatic:** Arabica.
- **Not sure:** buy one bag of each, brew them the same way, taste side by side and log both in your **Journal**.
