> [!NOTE]
> **Result:** about 100 ml of strong coffee, enough for one iced milk coffee or one bold black cup. Suits Robusta or a Robusta–Arabica blend at medium to dark roast.

## 1. How a phin works

A phin is a small metal cup with a perforated base, a press disc that sits on the coffee, and a lid. Water soaks through the bed and drips into the glass. No paper, no power — but because the holes are fixed, **grind size and press pressure** are the only ways to control the flow.

There are two kinds of press: **screw-down** (adjustable tightness) and **gravity** (it rests on the bed under its own weight). The gravity type is easier and more consistent when you're starting out.

## 2. Recipe

```brew-calc
method: phin
coffee: 20
ratio: 6
range: 3-10
```

- Grind **medium-fine**, like table salt. Too fine and water barely moves; too coarse and the coffee rushes through weak.
- Water just off the boil, rested about 30 seconds (roughly 92–96 °C).
- For black coffee without milk, stretch to about 1:8–1:10.

## 3. Steps

```brew-timer
method: phin
coffee: 20
water: 120
step: 0:00 | 40 | Bloom: just cover the grounds, set the press on top
step: 0:40 | 80 | Fill the chamber, put the lid on to keep heat in
step: 1:30 | | First drips should fall around now
step: 5:00 | | Last drips: lift the phin off
```

1. Rinse the phin and glass with hot water.
2. Add the coffee, shake to level it, and set the phin on the glass (with condensed milk already in it for a milk coffee).
3. Follow the timer above. The whole brew should take about **4–5 minutes**.

> [!TIP]
> No drips after 2 minutes? Grind coarser next time (or loosen a screw press). All done in under 3 minutes? Grind finer.

## 4. Cà phê sữa đá (iced milk coffee)

Put 1–2 spoons of sweetened condensed milk in the glass before brewing. When it's done, stir well and pour over a glass full of ice. How much milk is a matter of taste — start low and add more.

## 5. A note on "traditional" coffee

Some traditional Vietnamese roasters take Robusta very dark and may add butter, sugar or flavorings during roasting, which gives the taste many cafés are known for. To taste the bean itself, look for bags labelled **100% pure coffee** with a roast date.
