> [!NOTE]
> **Result:** about 600 ml of concentrate. Dilute 1:1 with water or milk over ice. Suits medium to dark roasts with chocolate and nutty notes.

## 1. Cold brew vs iced coffee

**Iced coffee** is brewed hot, then chilled. **Cold brew** uses no heat: cold water extracts far more slowly, so it needs hours of steeping. The low temperature pulls out fewer sour and bitter compounds, so the concentrate is usually smooth, sweet and easy to drink.

## 2. Recipe

```brew-calc
method: cold-brew
coffee: 100
ratio: 8
```

- Grind **coarse**, like sea salt. Fine grounds make it cloudy and hard to filter.
- Use filtered water at room temperature or cold.

## 3. Steps

```brew-timer
method: cold-brew
coffee: 100
water: 800
step: 0:00 | 800 | Mix coffee and water, stir until evenly wet, cover
step: 0:05:00 | | Stir gently once more, then refrigerate
step: 16:00:00 | | Strain through a paper or cloth filter
```

A steep of **12–24 hours** is typical ([Coffee Brews Hub](https://coffeebrewshub.com/blog/science-of-coffee-extraction)). The timer above defaults to 16 hours; it only runs while the page is open, so for cold brew a phone alarm is more practical.

> [!TIP]
> Longer steep → stronger and more bitter. For a lighter brew, shorten the time rather than cutting the coffee.

## 4. Storing and serving

- Keep the concentrate in a sealed glass bottle in the fridge, and drink it within a few days for the best aroma.
- To serve: 1 part concentrate + 1 part water or milk, over ice. Adjust to taste.
