Green coffee barely smells like coffee. In the roaster, sugars and amino acids react and caramelise, creating hundreds of aromatic compounds. The bean dries out, turns from green to yellow to brown, and swells.

## The two "cracks"

- **First crack:** steam and gas inside the bean split it with a popcorn-like crackle. From here the coffee is drinkable.
- **Second crack:** quieter, faster snapping as the bean's structure starts to break down; oils start to reach the surface. Past second crack is dark-roast territory.

## Three roast levels

| Roast | Stopped around | Typical taste | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Just after first crack | Bright acidity, floral, fruity | Pour-over, AeroPress |
| Medium | Between first and second crack | Balanced, caramel, milk chocolate | Most methods |
| Dark | Around or after second crack | Bitter, smoky, dark chocolate, low acidity | Phin, espresso with milk |

## Why beans need to rest

Freshly roasted beans hold a lot of CO₂. Gas escaping as water hits the grounds pushes water away and makes extraction uneven. Resting the beans a few days makes brewing more even — which is why the **Bean shelf** flags bags roasted less than four days ago.
