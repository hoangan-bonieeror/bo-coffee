A cup of black coffee is more than 98% water, and an espresso about 90% ([La Cabra](https://us.lacabra.com/pages/water)). If your water smells of chlorine or tastes odd, so will the coffee.

## Which water

- **Use:** water from a filter jug or under-sink filter, or a lightly mineralised bottled water.
- **Avoid:** chlorinated tap water; distilled or "pure" RO water — without minerals, water struggles to pull flavor out and coffee turns flat and sharp.

The Specialty Coffee Association's brewing-water standard targets total dissolved solids of about 150 mg/L, with 75–250 mg/L acceptable ([SCA Coffee Standards](https://sca.coffee/s/Coffee-Standards-1.pdf)). No need to measure — just remember that water with *some* minerals beats perfectly pure water.

## Temperature

Most coffee brews well with water around **90–96 °C**. Hotter water extracts faster.

> [!TIP]
> No temperature-control kettle? Boil, then wait about 30 seconds. Use slightly cooler water for dark roasts, hotter for light roasts.

The **Ratio calculator** in every brew guide shows a suggested temperature for each method.
